Warm-up — do this first

  1. Raise your temperature — 5 min easy cardio (bike, incline walk or rower) until you break a light sweat.
  2. Loosen the day's joints — 5-8 slow reps each. Upper-body days: band pull-aparts, shoulder dislocates, arm circles, cat-cow. Lower-body days: leg swings front & side, hip circles, 10 bodyweight squats, walking lunges.
  3. Ramp up the first lift only — empty bar ×10, then ~50% ×5, ~70% ×3, ~85% ×1. Rest 60-90 s and stop each set well short of effort.
  4. Start working set 1. Isolation moves (raises, curls, extensions) need just one light feeder set.

Important Notes

  • Weekly layout: Sat Lower A · Sun Upper A · Tue Lower B · Wed Upper B. Other days: rest or a walk — keep at least one full rest day.
  • Goal — lean & tone: the lifting keeps your muscle while a modest calorie deficit does the fat loss. The short rests already lift your heart rate; keep the reps controlled, don't turn every set into a race.
  • Conditioning: 7-9k steps a day, plus 2-3 brisk or incline walks of 25-35 min. Optional 6-8 min finisher after Lower B or Upper B — 40 s work / 20 s rest: goblet squats, RDLs, jumping jacks, mountain climbers.
  • Progression: add reps to the top of the range first, then a little weight. Keep 1-2 reps in reserve.
  • Form first: single-leg moves (split squat, single-leg RDL) are about control — go light until your balance is steady.
  • Warm-up: follow the warm-up card at the top of each day before your first set.
  • Nutrition: follow your meal-timing plan; aim for ~1.6-2 g protein per kg of body weight daily.

Important Notes

  • Weekly schedule: Sat Legs · Sun Push · Mon Pull · Tue rest (football) · Wed Lower · Thu Upper · Fri rest. Every muscle 2×/week with 3-4 days between; both rest days follow an upper day so football fits Tuesday; the heavy barbell squat + RDL sit on Legs day while Lower day stays spine-friendly (hack squat, hip thrust).
  • Exercise swaps: machine and cable picks can be swapped for the nearest equivalent your gym has. If shoulders lag, trade the Push cable fly for a machine shoulder press; if lower back is fried, drop a set from the RDL.
  • Progressive overload: add a little weight or a rep whenever you hit the top of the rep range with good form — aim to beat last session's numbers (shown on each card).
  • Train close to failure: stop most working sets 1-3 reps before failure; on isolation moves (raises, curls, extensions, calves) push to 0-1.
  • Warm-up: follow the warm-up card at the top of each day before your first set.
  • Core: trained 3×/week — hanging leg raise (Pull, lower abs), Pallof press (Upper, anti-rotation), weighted cable crunch (Lower, upper abs). Brace as if bracing for a punch; move through the abs, don't yank with the hip flexors. Add load once you clear the top of the rep range.
  • Recover: 7-9 h sleep — muscle is built between sessions. Football counts as leg load, so keep it to Tuesday and off the day before Legs.
  • Deload: every 5-8 weeks take an easy week (about ⅔ the weight or half the sets) to clear fatigue and keep progressing.
  • Protein: 1.6-2.2 g per kg of body weight daily, spread across 3-5 meals.
